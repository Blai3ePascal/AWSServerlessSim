## Structural results

### Layer count grows sharply from d=3 to d=5

The number of Trellis layers per shot is fixed for a circuit and was identical across shots and ranking modes:

| Family | d=3 layers | d=5 layers | Growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Surface | 219 | 1677 | 7.66x |
| Midout color | 143 | 1028 | 7.19x |
| Superdense color | 420 | 3128 | 7.45x |

At d=5, superdense traverses about 1.87x as many layers as surface and 3.04x as many as midout.

### Frontier width also grows, but topology matters independently of layer count

Maximum active frontier width:

| Family | d=3 | d=5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Surface | 12 | 29 |
| Midout color | 7 | 17 |
| Superdense color | 12 | 29 |

The d=3 to d=5 frontier maximum grows by about 2.42x in all three families.

Surface and superdense have the same maximum frontier at each tested distance, but superdense has many more layers. Therefore maximum frontier alone is not a sufficient workload descriptor; both frontier pressure and traversal length must be retained in later models.

### Beam 64 is already close to saturation for d=5

Using ranking mode `mass` as a representative reference, mean `beam_in` divided by the configured cap 64 was:

- surface d=5: 98.85%;
- midout d=5: 97.33%;
- superdense d=5: 99.17%.

Median beam size was 64 for every tested cell, including d=3, although the d=3 means show more unsaturated early/late layers. Midout d=3 has the lowest mean beam occupancy, about 44.0 states, and is therefore a useful low-pressure control.

The maximum post-collapse state count was 128 and maximum retained count was 64 in every cell. This is consistent with the two-branch expansion reaching roughly twice the beam before truncation in at least some layers.

### Ranking mode changes structure only slightly in this small beam-64 sample

For a fixed circuit, the three ranking modes produce identical layer count and frontier geometry. Their mean state-population metrics differ only modestly in this 3-shot sample.

The spread in mean `states_after_collapse` between ranking modes is approximately:

- surface d=3: 0.52%;
- surface d=5: 0.48%;
- midout d=3: 0.50%;
- midout d=5: 1.02%;
- superdense d=3: 0.33%;
- superdense d=5: 0.66%.

This is not evidence that ranking mode is unimportant. Beam 64 is heavily saturated for d=5 and the sample contains only three shots. It does suggest that the next controlled experiment should vary beam width before spending a large matrix on ranking modes.

## Interpretation

P03 supports four decisions for P04:

1. **Vary beam width.** A single beam=64 point is insufficient because d=5 spends most layers near the cap.
2. **Keep topology in the workload model.** Superdense and surface can share maximum frontier width while differing strongly in number of layers.
3. **Use midout d=3 as a control workload.** It exercises Trellis without the same degree of beam saturation.
4. **Freeze syndromes before benchmarking.** Ranking-mode comparisons must see byte-identical input shots so decoder policy is not confounded by sampling variance.
